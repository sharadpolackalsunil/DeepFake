\section{Pre-Registered Statistical Hypotheses and Audits}
\label{sec:stats}

To prevent opportunistic metric reporting and establish rigorous inferential validity, we evaluate three pre-registered statistical hypotheses governing architectural utility, operating point transfer, and operational prior shift.

\subsection{Hypothesis H1: Paired Cluster Bootstrap $\Delta\text{AUC}$ and Equivalence Testing}
\paragraph{Formulation.}
For any model pair $(A, B)$, we evaluate whether model $A$ is statistically superior to model $B$ beyond an equivalence margin $\delta = 0.01$:
\begin{align}
H_0: \Delta\text{AUC} \le \delta \quad \text{vs.} \quad H_1: \Delta\text{AUC} > \delta
\end{align}
We compute two-sided confidence intervals using iterations of the \textbf{Paired Cluster Bootstrap}. Crucially, bootstrap sampling is performed at the \emph{cluster level} rather than across individual video clips, resampling 140 video clusters on FF++, 124 celebrity identity clusters on Celeb-DF v2, 273 scene/actor clusters on pure DFD ($N=2{,}847$), and $991$ clusters on FakeAVCeleb ($499$ authentic identities, $492$ fake clusters). For confirmatory testing on FakeAVCeleb, we compute $N_{\text{boot}} = 10{,}000$ iterations with Bonferroni family adjustment ($m=2 \rightarrow 97.5\%$ CI, level $0.975$). This guarantees cluster-correlated errors between identical actors or underlying source sequences do not artificially inflate statistical significance.

\paragraph{Statistical Conclusions (H1 Supported).}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:h1_bootstrap}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Confirmatory Equivalence on FakeAVCeleb (H1 Supported):} On the pre-registered confirmatory benchmark FakeAVCeleb ($991$ independent clusters, $2{,}998$ videos), both confirmatory tests fall strictly within the equivalence bounds $[-\delta, +\delta]$ ($\delta=0.01$):
    \begin{align}
    \Delta\text{AUC}(\texttt{smsdt\_st} - \texttt{smsdt\_s}) &= -0.0045, \quad 97.5\%\text{ CI }[-0.0096, +0.0006] \implies \textbf{\texttt{equivalent}} \\
    \Delta\text{AUC}(\texttt{smsdt\_full} - \texttt{smsdt\_s}) &= +0.0012, \quad 97.5\%\text{ CI }[-0.0050, +0.0073] \implies \textbf{\texttt{equivalent}}
    \end{align}
    This is consistent with our primary architectural hypothesis: \textbf{on this benchmark, adding multi-frame spatio-temporal attention or dual forensic residual streams yields no measurable generalization AUC advantage over single-frame PEFT} within a $\pm 0.01$ margin across modern manipulation methods.
    
    \paragraph{Seed Variance and Scope Caveat.}
    We note that these cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals resample test clusters only, reflecting test-set sampling uncertainty rather than training-seed variance. The seed-to-seed standard deviation of \texttt{smsdt\_st} on FakeAVCeleb is $\sigma_{\text{seed}} = 0.0080$, which is comparable to the equivalence margin $\delta = 0.01$. Across individual training seeds, the paired differences for \texttt{ST - S} are $-0.0152$ (seed 1, slightly outside $[-\delta, +\delta]$), $+0.0037$ (seed 2), and $-0.0021$ (seed 3); for \texttt{Full - S}, they are $-0.0009$, $-0.0005$, and $+0.0050$. Equivalence is therefore formally established for the pooled evaluation across this benchmark ($83.4\%$ fake prevalence, VoxCeleb2 source reals, 5 manipulation algorithms).
    \item \textbf{Resolving Underpowered Development Splits:} On Celeb-DF v2 ($179$ clusters), the confidence intervals for \texttt{Full vs S} ($[-0.0142, +0.0262]$) and \texttt{ST vs S} ($[-0.0312, +0.0068]$) spanned outside $[-\delta, +\delta]$, correctly classifying the development split as underpowered. FakeAVCeleb's $991$ independent clusters provided the necessary power to resolve equivalence. On DFD ($273$ pure clusters), \texttt{smsdt\_full} scored lower than \texttt{smsdt\_s} ($-0.0192$, \texttt{worse}), reinforcing that heavier heads do not improve cross-domain transfer.
    \item \textbf{Recipe vs. Architecture Disentanglement (Gate G1 Branch B):} Comparing \texttt{smsdt\_s} against \texttt{gend\_ours} shows a large, consistent delta across all datasets ($+0.0530$ on FakeAVCeleb, $+0.0532$ on Celeb-DF v2, $+0.0583$ on FF++), demonstrating that training recipe, curriculum scheduling, and loss formulation drive substantial performance gains under identical pretrained backbones. Furthermore, auditing published GenD (\texttt{gend\_official}) shows that it outperforms every S-MSDT variant on out-of-domain benchmarks ($0.9745$ on FakeAVCeleb, $0.9327$ on Celeb-DF), demonstrating the potency of full author pretraining.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hypothesis H2: Operating Point Transfer and Degradation Gap $g$}
\paragraph{Formulation.}
When deploying a deepfake detector, the decision threshold $\tau_{\text{val}}$ must be fixed prior to deployment (e.g., via Youden's $J$ statistic on validation data). Hypothesis H2 pre-registered that validation-frozen operating cutoffs would experience catastrophic operating point failure under cross-dataset domain shift, formulated as a degradation gap:
\begin{equation}
g = \text{HTER}(\tau_{\text{val}}) - \min_{\tau} \text{HTER}(\tau) \ge 0.05
\end{equation}
exceeding 5.0\% HTER on unseen evaluation datasets.

\paragraph{Audit Results (H2 Falsified).}
As reported across all individual runs in Table~\ref{tab:master_matrix}, the empirical degradation gap remains uniformly small across all 36 evaluated conditions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item In-domain FF++: $g \in [0.0000, 0.0121]$ (average $0.0061$).
    \item Cross-dataset Celeb-DF v2: $g \in [0.0029, 0.0374]$ (average $0.0118$).
    \item Cross-dataset DFD: $g \in [0.0003, 0.0173]$ (average $0.0053$).
\end{itemize}
Because $g \le 0.0374 \ll 0.05$ across every condition, Hypothesis H2 is formally \textbf{falsified}. This falsification delivers an important scientific insight: cross-dataset operational errors are not primarily driven by threshold miscalibration ($g$ is negligible), but rather by rank-order score overlap and false positive rate inflation on authentic out-of-domain faces.

\subsection{Hypothesis H3: Operational Prior Shift and Saerens EM Correction}
\paragraph{Formulation.}
In production environments, the base rate of synthetic content $\pi = P(Y=1)$ deviates unpredictably from training class balance ($0.50$). Hypothesis H3 evaluates detector resilience under simulated prior shifts $\pi \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$ on Celeb-DF v2 (50 Monte Carlo resamplings per condition) and tests whether unsupervised Saerens Expectation-Maximization (EM) \citep{saerens2002adjusting} accurately estimates the true operational prior:
\begin{equation}
p^{(k+1)}(Y=1|x) \propto \frac{\pi^{(k)}}{0.50} \cdot p(Y=1|x)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) and Equilibrium Collapse Audit}
\label{sec:tta_audit}
\paragraph{Formulation.}
To investigate whether unsupervised test-time optimization can repair transferred representations without labels, we evaluate LayerNorm entropy minimization \citep{wang2021tent} at learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$. We compare:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Online Streaming TTA:} Continuous adaptation where parameters update sequentially across the unseen test video stream.
    \item \textbf{Batch-Episodic TTA:} Parameters are updated strictly on the current test mini-batch, predictions are recorded, and LayerNorm weights are immediately reset to their source state.
\end{enumerate}
Following pre-registration, we flag an operating failure as \emph{Equilibrium Collapse} if post-adaptation AUC degrades by $> 0.03$ or if predicted positive share diverges to $>95\%$ or $<5\%$ under balanced ground truth.

\paragraph{Empirical Findings.}
As revealed in Table~\ref{tab:tta_benchmark}, unconstrained online entropy minimization suffers catastrophic failure on deepfake test streams. In online mode, prediction entropy declines rapidly, driving logits into a degenerate trivial attractor where nearly all test samples are classified as authentic ($6.6\%$ positive on Celeb-DF, $0.6\%$ on DFD). \subsection{Hypothesis H7: Split-Conformal Real-Shot Budget Transfer Benchmark (Confirmatory Validation)}
\label{sec:real_shot}
\paragraph{Formulation and Pre-Registration Protocol.}
Because frozen source quantiles fail under severe distribution shifts (e.g., $15.7\%$ Celeb-DF FPR on official GenD) and label-free target GMMs collapse under prior skew, we established and pre-registered the \textbf{real-shot budget transfer benchmark}. We evaluate whether calibrating on a small budget of $n$ identity-disjoint pristine target reals can guarantee target false alarm control ($\le \alpha + 0.01$) while retaining $\ge 75\%$ of oracle recall.

Prior to scoring any checkpoint, the evaluation manifest was frozen at Git commit \texttt{bd90d08} (tag \texttt{prereg-freeze-h7}), isolating $499$ pristine VoxCeleb2 identities (1 video/ID) and $2{,}499$ visual fakes across 5 manipulation methods on FakeAVCeleb\_v1.2. Checkpoints were scored blindly, and unopened scores were committed at commit \texttt{f2bd09b}.

Given $n$ calibration reals with anomaly scores $s_1, \dots, s_n$, we apply \textbf{split-conformal quantile calibration} \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}:
\begin{align}
\tau_{\text{conf}} = s_{(k)}, \quad \text{where } k = \lceil (1 - \alpha)(n + 1) \rceil
\end{align}
For $n=20$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $k = \lceil 0.95 \times 21 \rceil = 20 = n$, which corresponds to the maximum calibration score. We compare conformal calibration against naive empirical quantiles ($k = \lceil (1-\alpha)n \rceil$) and parametric adaptive shift estimation across 500 random identity calibration draws.

\subsection{Hypothesis H8: Confirmatory Audit of the Real-Shot Budget Certificate}
\label{sec:h8_audit}

\paragraph{Formulation and Closed-Form Beta-Binomial Law.}
While split-conformal calibration guarantees that the population expectation $\mathbb{E}[\text{FPR}] \le \alpha$, in any real-world deployment or finite benchmark, performance is evaluated on a finite held-out set of $m$ authentic identities. Hypothesis H8 evaluates our parameter-free \textbf{Real-Shot Budget Certificate}. Under identity-level exchangeability, the number of false alarms $X$ on $m$ held-out identities follows an exact Beta-Binomial distribution:
\begin{equation}
X \sim \text{BetaBinomial}(m, \; n + 1 - k, \; k), \quad \text{where } k = \lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil
\end{equation}
Consequently, the probability that the evaluated false alarm rate exceeds $2\alpha$ is predicted analytically in closed form by:
\begin{equation}
P\left(\frac{X}{m} > 2\alpha\right) = 1 - F_{\text{BB}}(\lfloor 2\alpha m \rfloor; \; m, \; n + 1 - k, \; k)
\end{equation}
where $F_{\text{BB}}$ is the cumulative distribution function of the Beta-Binomial law. Furthermore, we pre-registered five analytical ``sweet-spot'' sample sizes where $\mathbb{E}[\text{FPR}] = \alpha$ exactly: $n \in \{19, 39, 59\}$ for $\alpha=0.05$ (where $k = n$) and $n \in \{9, 29\}$ for $\alpha=0.10$. Between sweet spots (e.g., $n=30$ at $\alpha=0.05$), the threshold becomes conservative ($\mathbb{E}[\text{FPR}] = 0.0322$), needlessly sacrificing detection recall.

\paragraph{Audit Findings.}
As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:h8_summary}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Registered verdict: H8 falsified.} H8 was pre-registered (decision D20, tag \texttt{prereg-freeze-h8}) as supported only if both (i) at least $90\%$ of cells have the observed $\hat{P}(\text{FPR} > 2\alpha)$ inside the $99\%$ binomial interval of the prediction, and (ii) mean FPR $\le \alpha + 2\,\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{MC}}$ in \emph{every} cell. Criterion (i) passes in 130 of 130 cells. Criterion (ii) passes in 124 of 130, so the registered verdict is \textbf{falsified}.
    \item \textbf{Where validity fails.} All 6 failing cells are on \texttt{fakeavceleb} and involve \texttt{smsdt\_s} (seed 1) and \texttt{smsdt\_st} (seeds 1, 2). Mean FPR exceeds the budget by $0.0020$ to $0.0062$ (for example $0.1062$ against $\alpha=0.10$ at $n=9$ for \texttt{smsdt\_st} seed 2), which is $2.0$ to $2.7$ Monte Carlo standard errors. About 3 of 130 cells would exceed $2\,\mathrm{SE}$ by chance under a one-sided test, so this is a small excess and not evidence of a large miscalibration; we did not test for a cause, and non-exchangeability between calibration and evaluation identities is one possible explanation. An exploratory post-hoc check (not pre-registered) shows all 130 cells within $3\,\mathrm{SE}$ (largest $2.7$).
    \item \textbf{Prediction fidelity (supported part).} Closed-form predictions match the observed exceedance rates closely: on \texttt{fakeavceleb} at $n=39$ the formula gives $0.0906$ and the mean observed rate is $0.0898$; on \texttt{celebdf} at $n=39$ it gives $0.1072$ against $0.1068$. Mean empirical FPR is $0.0489$ to $0.0514$ for $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.0983$ to $0.1022$ for $\alpha=0.10$.
    \item \textbf{Sample-size planning.} The planner is a calculation from the formula, and it depends on the held-out size $m$. For $m$ near $450$ identities (as on \texttt{fakeavceleb}), $n=39$ gives $P(\text{FPR} > 2\alpha) \approx 0.09$ and $n=79$ gives about $0.045$. On \texttt{celebdf}, where $m$ is only $82$ at $n=39$, the value is $0.107$, above $0.10$. Planner statements therefore need $m$ to be quoted alongside $n$.
    \item \textbf{Operational Triage Policy:} Deploying dual certificates at alert budget $\alpha=0.05$ and review budget $\beta=0.10$ establishes an automated three-way triage pipeline (\emph{Flag} / \emph{Review} / \emph{Pass}), directing borderline cases to human verification while clearing authentic uploads with certified guarantees.
\end{enumerate}
